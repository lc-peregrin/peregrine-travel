Part 1: Target List

How to read this list

You need one of three things from a partner: (a) GDS booking credentials
as a sub-agent, (b) a ticketing/hold arrangement where they create PNRs
on your behalf, or (c) API access that supports unticketed holds. Tier 1
is where the deal most likely gets done; Tiers 2–3 are for redundancy
and education.

Tier 1 — Regional consolidators & sub-agency arrangements (highest probability of “yes”)

These businesses live on volume from sub-agents and are commercially
pragmatic. The non-IATA sub-agent model (you book on their credentials,
pay per segment) is standard practice across South/Southeast Asia.

Tier 4 — The quiet route (potentially the real answer)

Find the agency that already supplies an existing onward-ticket service.
Several mid-size Asian consolidators quietly run this volume today. Two
ways in: (1) ask Tier 1 targets directly — “do you already serve clients
in the proof-of-onward-travel space?”; (2) look at the issuing office /
booking origin details on competitor sample PNRs (their FAQ screenshots
and Trustpilot reviews sometimes reveal airline and region patterns). An
agency already absorbing this risk for one client will price a second
client efficiently.

Part 2: Qualification Questions (ask on the first call)

1.  Can you create GDS reservations that hold unticketed for 24–72h, and
    on which airlines/booking classes?
2.  What do you charge per segment for bookings that never ticket? (If
    they say “nothing, holds are free” — they haven’t thought about it;
    walk them to a per-segment fee so the relationship survives contact
    with reality.)
3.  What’s your churn tolerance before ADMs/GDS unproductive-segment
    fees become your problem — and can we structure the fee so I’m
    pre-paying that risk?
4.  Can holds be created via API/robotics on your credentials, or manual
    queue only?
5.  Have you had ADM issues before? Which airlines enforce hardest in
    your market?
6.  Can you also ticket-and-void (24h US DOT window / void windows in
    BSP) for a premium product?
7.  What monthly volume makes this worth your time — and what volume
    makes you nervous?

Deal shape to aim for: USD $0.75–2.00 per segment created, prepaid in
blocks, no exclusivity, 30-day exit both ways, and an explicit written
understanding of what the bookings are for. Two signed partners before
launch, not one.

Part 3: Supply Pitch Email

Version A — Direct/honest (default; use for Tier 1 Asia)

Subject: Commercial proposal — recurring GDS segment volume, unusual use
case

Hi [Name],

I’m an Australian business developer building a proof-of-onward-travel
service — the product travellers buy when an airline or immigration
officer requires evidence of an onward flight (similar to
onwardticket.com, which does this at significant scale today).

I’m writing to you openly about what this involves, because the
partnership only works if it’s built on that: I need the ability to
create genuine GDS reservations at volume, most of which will
intentionally never be ticketed and will lapse at the hold deadline. I
understand this is churn, that churn has a cost to you (ADM exposure,
GDS unproductive segment fees), and I want to pay for it properly rather
than pretend it isn’t there.

What I’m proposing: - A per-segment fee for every reservation created,
prepaid in blocks — you price your risk in - Volume starting at
~[300–500] segments/month, scaling with my sales - Booking distribution
spread across carriers/classes to your guidance, keeping per-airline
churn below enforcement attention - No exclusivity, 30-day exit for both
sides

If you already serve a client in this space, even better — you know the
economics work. Could we set up a 20-minute call this week or next?

Regards, Liam Conroy [phone / LinkedIn]

Version B — Softer opener (use for Tier 2 Australia, where compliance culture is stiffer)

Subject: Sub-agency enquiry — niche travel documentation product

Hi [Name],

I’m launching a travel documentation service for the visa and
proof-of-onward-travel market and looking for a consolidator partner for
GDS reservation capability. The commercial model is unusual — a high
volume of short-hold reservations with a low ticketing ratio — so rather
than squeeze it into a standard sub-agency arrangement, I’d prefer to
design a per-segment fee structure that compensates you fairly for what
the volume actually is.

Happy to walk through the full model transparently on a short call —
would [day] or [day] suit?

Regards, Liam

Follow-up sequence

-   Day 3: one-liner bump (“Any interest in a quick call on the below?
    The volume is real and I’m signing two partners this month.”)
-   Day 8: add proof/urgency (“For context — the market leader in this
    niche does ~170K site visits/month; I’m building the API/white-label
    layer of it. If holds aren’t a fit for your operation, would
    ticket-and-void be?”)
-   Day 15: breakup email (“Closing out my partner shortlist this week —
    if the timing’s wrong I’ll stop here, but happy to revisit later.”)

Part 4: B2B White-Label Pitch (run in parallel, weeks 2–3)

Subject: Add proof-of-onward-travel to [Product] — rev share, one API
call

Hi [Name],

Your users hit the proof-of-onward-travel problem constantly — [visa-run
customers / travellers activating eSIMs at borders / policyholders
crossing into Thailand and Indonesia]. Right now they bounce out to
onwardticket.com and pay a stranger $16.

I’m building a white-label API for exactly this: your brand, one API
call, a verifiable airline reservation delivered to your user in under a
minute. You keep [30–40]% of each sale, zero inventory or support burden
on your side.

I’m signing 3 launch partners before the public release. Worth a
15-minute call to see if [Product] should be one?

Regards, Liam

Target categories for this pitch: visa agencies (Bangkok, Bali, HCMC,
Manila), border-run/visa-run tour operators, eSIM apps, nomad insurance
products, coworking/nomad community platforms, travel agencies
specialising in Schengen visa applications (India, Philippines, LATAM).
