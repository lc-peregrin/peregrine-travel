Onward Ticket Service — Business Plan

Prepared July 2026 · All competitor figures are third-party estimates
and should be validated with SimilarWeb/Ahrefs before committing capital

------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. The Business in One Paragraph

Sell verifiable flight reservations (real airline PNRs, never ticketed)
for USD $14–20 to travellers who need proof of onward travel for airline
check-in, immigration, or visa applications. Marginal cost per unit is
near zero (a GDS booking hold), fulfilment is fully automatable, and
demand is structural — it exists as long as visa-exempt entry rules
require onward proof. The business is a margin machine constrained not
by demand or technology but by supply access (GDS/host agency
relationships) and distribution (SEO trust).

Revenue back-of-envelope

Traffic here is extremely high-intent — people arrive from searches like
“onward ticket,” “dummy ticket for visa,” or Reddit referrals with
wallet already open. Reasonable conversion assumptions are 4–10% (vs
1–3% for generic e-commerce).

onwardticket.com: - Conservative: 170K × 4% × $17 AOV ≈ $115K/month
(~$1.4M/yr) - Mid: 170K × 7% × $18 AOV (incl. upsells) ≈ $215K/month
(~$2.6M/yr) - Aggressive: 170K × 10% × $20 ≈ $340K/month (~$4M/yr)

Given near-zero COGS, operating margin plausibly 60–80% after payment
fees, agency costs, support staff, and marketing. The leader is likely a
$1.5–4M revenue, $1–3M profit business run by a handful of people.

Tier-2 players (50K visits): ~$35–90K/month revenue. Even the 27K-visit
challenger likely clears $20–50K/month.

Total addressable direct market: roughly $6–12M/yr today, growing with
visa-run tourism, digital nomadism, and increasingly automated airline
document checks. The larger adjacent market is bundled
proof-of-onward-travel inside visa agencies, eSIM apps, and travel
insurance products.

3. What It Would Take to Build

Critical path: booking supply (the real barrier)

Options, in ascending order of robustness:

1.  Partner with a host agency / consolidator. You operate as a
    sub-agent; they hold IATA accreditation and GDS contracts
    (Amadeus/Sabre/Travelport). Fastest route (weeks). Risk: they will
    cut you off once churn volume triggers airline ADM fines.
    Mitigation: be honest upfront, negotiate a per-segment fee that
    compensates their risk, spread volume across 2–3 agencies.
2.  Non-IATA agency with GDS access via a ticketing consolidator. Common
    in Asia (Thailand, India, Philippines). More control, moderate setup
    (1–3 months).
3.  Own IATA/TIDS accreditation + direct GDS contracts. 6–12 months,
    capital requirements, and airlines will still ADM you for churning.
    This is where incumbents likely sit, with sophisticated
    hold-rotation logic (booking across many airlines, keeping churn per
    airline below enforcement thresholds).
4.  Buy-and-cancel fallback. Use fares with free 24h cancellation (US
    DOT rule) or void windows. Higher working capital, real cash
    cycling, but bulletproof verifiability. bestonwardticket’s model.
    Good as a premium tier.

A durable operation probably runs 1 + 4 hybrid: cheap holds for the
standard product, buy-and-cancel for a “guaranteed ticketed” premium
tier.

Technology (trivial — 4–8 weeks)

-   Next.js storefront (checkout form: name, route preference or
    auto-assign, date, validity)
-   Booking automation: agency API or GDS API (Amadeus Self-Service APIs
    exist; hold capability depends on contract) or, crudely, a
    human-in-the-loop agent dashboard at low volume
-   PDF itinerary generation with live PNR the customer can verify on
    the airline site
-   Auto-expiry/rebooking engine for extended-validity products
-   Email delivery, order status, refund flow

Payments

High-risk classification is likely. Plan A: Stripe with careful merchant
categorisation and low dispute rate. Plan B: high-risk processors
(e.g. travel-specialised PSPs), plus crypto as a secondary rail (nomad
audience accepts it). Keep chargebacks under 1% via instant delivery +
responsive support.

Distribution (the moat you must build)

-   SEO: “onward ticket,” “dummy ticket,” “flight itinerary for visa,”
    plus country-specific long-tail (“proof of onward travel Thailand,”
    “Schengen visa flight reservation”). Incumbents own the head terms;
    entry is via long-tail country/visa pages (200+ programmatic pages)
    and non-English markets (Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Bahasa —
    SimilarWeb shows incumbents already ranking on “vuelos
    baratos”-adjacent terms, i.e. LATAM demand is real and underserved).
-   Reddit/forum reputation: r/digitalnomad, r/travel, r/Thailand,
    VisaJourney, nomad Facebook groups. Slow, authentic, decisive — this
    is where purchase decisions actually happen.
-   Affiliate/creator program: travel YouTubers and visa-run bloggers,
    20–30% commission. Incumbents underuse this.
-   B2B embed: white-label API for visa agencies, eSIM apps, travel
    insurers. One integration can equal thousands of retail orders and
    is far more defensible.

4. Risks (ranked)

1.  Supply cutoff. Airlines fine agencies for churn (ADMs); your host
    agency drops you. Mitigation: multi-agency redundancy, hold-rotation
    logic, buy-and-cancel fallback tier.
2.  Regulatory/policy shift. Airlines or IATA tighten hold rules (some
    airlines already require instant ticketing on certain fares); a
    country starts verifying ticketed status. Mitigation: premium
    ticketed tier already built.
3.  Payment processor termination. Mitigation: two PSPs from day one +
    crypto rail; keep disputes <1%.
4.  Legal/reputational grey zone. The reservation is real, but customers
    use it to satisfy immigration requirements they don’t intend to
    honour. Not clearly illegal anywhere major, but get a legal opinion
    in your operating jurisdiction, keep ToS explicit (“verifiable
    reservation, not travel”), and incorporate somewhere pragmatic (SG,
    HK, Estonia e-residency, or AU with advice).
5.  SEO dependence + Google/AI-search volatility. AI answers
    increasingly intercept “do I need an onward ticket” queries.
    Mitigation: brand searches, affiliates, B2B embed, email list.
6.  Incumbent moat. onwardticket.com has years of Trustpilot reviews and
    Reddit citations. Head-on competition on “onward ticket” is a losing
    fight for 2+ years.

5. Strategic Verdict

Don’t clone the leader; flank it. Three viable wedges:

1.  Geographic/language wedge: Spanish/Portuguese/Bahasa/Hindi-first
    product for LATAM and Asian outbound travellers — demonstrably
    underserved, incumbents’ non-English SEO is thin.
2.  Premium trust wedge: “actually ticketed, guaranteed verifiable for
    48h, refund if it fails at check-in” at $29–39. Converts the
    paranoid segment incumbents leave anxious.
3.  B2B/API wedge: white-label onward-ticket API for visa agencies, eSIM
    and nomad-insurance apps. Higher defensibility, no SEO war,
    recurring volume.

The retail business is real (~$1.5–4M/yr for the leader, healthy five
figures monthly for tier-2 players) and technically easy. The genuine
difficulty is boring: agency relationships, payment rails, and two years
of trust-building. Anyone entering should budget for the moat-building,
not the code.

6. Recommendations

Overall call: pursue as a lean side bet, not a primary venture — and enter through the B2B door

The economics are genuinely attractive, but the retail path is a
two-year SEO trust war against entrenched incumbents with near-identical
products. That’s a grind with a binary payoff. The B2B/API wedge inverts
this: it plays to sales and relationship skills rather than content
marketing, it’s defensible, and five signed integration partners could
match the order volume of a year of organic SEO. Specific
recommendations, in priority order:

1. Validate supply before anything else (weeks 1–4, cost: ~$0). The
entire business hinges on GDS hold access. Before building anything,
have direct conversations with 3–5 travel consolidators/host agencies —
Thailand and the Philippines are strong hunting grounds for non-IATA
sub-agency arrangements, and being on the ground in SE Asia is a genuine
advantage here. The question to answer: will they knowingly support
hold-churn volume at an agreed per-segment fee, and at what threshold do
they get nervous? If nobody credible says yes, the venture dies cheaply.
If two say yes, there’s redundancy from day one.

2. Lead with the white-label API pitch, not a consumer site (months
1–3). Draft a one-page offer — “add verifiable proof-of-onward-travel to
your product, revenue share, one API call” — and pitch it to visa
agencies, eSIM apps, nomad insurance products, and visa-run tour
operators in Thailand/Vietnam/Indonesia. Even two LOIs before building
justifies the investment and effectively pre-sells the volume. This is a
cold-outreach and partnership sales problem, not a content-marketing
problem — a better fit for a BD skillset.

3. Build the thinnest possible MVP (months 2–3, <$5K). A checkout page,
manual fulfilment through the agency partner, PDF with live PNR, and
instant email delivery. Automate only after 200+ orders prove the
funnel. Next.js/Vercel is entirely sufficient. Don’t build the
auto-rebooking engine, multi-language site, or dashboards until revenue
demands it.

4. If pursuing retail at all, go non-English first. Spanish and Bahasa
Indonesia long-tail visa pages face a fraction of the competition of
English “onward ticket” terms. Treat English head terms as a 2+ year
project or skip them entirely.

5. Structure for the grey zone from day one. Incorporate deliberately
(Singapore or Estonia e-residency are pragmatic options; get proper tax
advice for the relevant residency), write ToS stating plainly that the
product is a verifiable reservation and not transportation, obtain a
short legal opinion in the operating jurisdiction, and run two payment
processors plus a crypto rail before scaling any ad spend. Businesses in
this niche die from processor termination and supply cutoff, not from
lack of customers.

6. Set a kill/scale threshold. Cap initial exposure at ~$15–20K and 6
months. Scale trigger: 1,000+ cumulative orders or one signed B2B
integration. Kill trigger: no agency willing to renew supply terms, or
sub-2% conversion on qualified traffic after 3 months of iteration. This
keeps it a cheap hypothesis test that can sit alongside employment and
other projects rather than becoming a sunk-cost trap.

What not to do: don’t clone the leader’s site and bid on “onward ticket”
keywords — $2.50–3+ CPCs against an incumbent with superior conversion
trust is negative-EV; don’t self-fund IATA accreditation before proving
demand; don’t launch without processor redundancy.
