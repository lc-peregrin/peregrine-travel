# Peregrin — Marketing Program

Prepared for Liam Conroy, 2026-07-20. Companion to the Business Model & Operations doc. Builds on the competitor/market research, B2B partner target list, and affiliate prep already completed.

## 1. Positioning: don't clone the leader, flank it

onwardticket.com and the other established players compete almost entirely on the same three trust signals — "real PNR," "verifiable on the airline's own site," and speed — because the core anxiety every buyer has is "will immigration reject this." Nobody wins on price alone. Going head-on at their SEO terms ("onward ticket," "dummy ticket") is a multi-year trust-and-content war against sites with years of Trustpilot reviews and Reddit citations — a fight not worth taking on directly yet.

Peregrin's positioning is built on three wedges instead, each avoiding that direct fight:

**B2B/API wedge (primary, start here).** Sell wholesale to travel agencies, visa consultancies, relocation firms, and eSIM/insurance apps who already own the traveller relationship. This isn't competing for individual travellers' trust — it's a sales conversation with a business, and it's already underway (shortlist and outreach built for Thailand/Philippines/Indonesia agencies).

**Language/geography wedge.** Incumbents' non-English SEO is thin — Spanish, Portuguese, Bahasa, and Hindi markets are demonstrably underserved despite real demand (LATAM search volume for flight/visa terms is already substantial). The product already ships in English, Spanish, Russian, and Hindi.

**Premium trust wedge.** A genuinely ticketed, refund-if-it-fails tier at $29–49 for the risk-averse segment that incumbents' cheaper unticketed product leaves anxious.

## 2. Target segments

**B2B partners (primary near-term focus):**
- Retail travel agencies in Southeast Asia (Thailand/TTAA, Philippines/PTAA, Indonesia/ASTINDO directories already mapped) who want to offer this to their own customers without building it themselves.
- Visa consultancies and processing services (iVisa-adjacent) — natural two-way referral partner, not just a sales target.
- Corporate relocation and immigration law firms — lower volume, higher value per client, recurring need.
- Existing "dummy ticket" resellers (e.g. FlyingHelpline's 500+ agent network) — potential wholesale customers if Peregrin can underprice their current GDS-access cost via direct Duffel integration.

**B2C travellers (secondary, longer runway):**
- Digital nomads and long-term travellers doing repeated visa runs (Thailand/Bali/Philippines/Vietnam corridor) — the classic "onward ticket" buyer, high purchase intent, price-sensitive, community-driven decision-making (Reddit, nomad Facebook groups).
- Tourist-visa applicants for stricter destinations (US, Schengen, Australia, New Zealand) needing proof of onward travel as part of a formal application.
- Non-English-speaking travellers underserved by incumbents' English-only SEO and support.

**Affiliate/referral partners (fast, low-effort, parallel track):**
- iVisa (visa processing, natural cross-referral both directions, ~20% commission, 365-day cookie).
- SafetyWing / World Nomads (travel insurance, same audience, ~10% of premium).
- eSIM/connectivity apps (Airalo-style) — common bundle for the same traveller segment.

## 3. Channels and sequencing

**Phase 1 — B2B outreach (already underway, keep pushing).** The shortlist, contact research, and compliant outreach copy already exist for Thai/Philippine/Indonesian agencies. This is the fastest realistic path to a first dollar because it only takes one partner saying yes, versus building organic traffic from zero. Priority: follow up on sent batches, expand the shortlist to additional countries (Malaysia, Vietnam) once the current batch has run its course, and start direct conversations with visa consultancies and relocation firms in parallel — these have longer sales cycles but higher per-client value.

**Phase 2 — Affiliate program activation.** Complete the iVisa and SafetyWing signups (prepped, just needs the user's own identity/financial details entered), get tracked referral links live, and wire them into the site and any partner-facing materials. Near-zero effort relative to payoff, and it validates the "visa-adjacent audience" thesis cheaply before investing further in retail acquisition.

**Phase 3 — Content/SEO (start now in parallel, pays off later).** This is the highest-leverage channel every competitor in this market runs on, and it has the longest lead time of anything in the plan — the case for starting it early is stronger than for almost anything else, precisely because it won't pay off for months. Recommended approach: skip the head terms incumbents already own ("onward ticket," "dummy ticket") and go straight for underserved long-tail and non-English pages:
- Country-specific guides in the language wedge markets — "prueba de viaje de continuación Tailandia," "требование обратного билета," Hindi-language visa-run guides — matched to the four languages already live on the product.
- Long-tail English pages by destination + visa type ("proof of onward travel Thailand," "Schengen visa flight reservation," "onward ticket Bali digital nomad visa").
- Structure as programmatic country/visa-type pages rather than one-off blog posts, so the page count can scale without linear content-writing effort.

**Phase 4 — Community and reputation.** Reddit (r/digitalnomad, r/travel, r/Thailand), VisaJourney, and nomad Facebook groups are where this category's purchase decisions actually get made — slower and less scalable than paid channels, but far more trusted, and cheap. Worth genuine participation (answering real questions, not drive-by promotion) once there's a live, working product to point to — which there now is.

**Phase 5 — Creator/affiliate program for the retail channel.** Travel YouTubers and visa-run bloggers at 20–30% commission — flagged in the original research as underused by incumbents, meaning less competition for the same creators' attention. Lower priority than the above until B2B and affiliate tracks are generating some validation.

**Deprioritised for now: paid acquisition.** High-intent categories like this often carry high-risk payments classification and thin margins on the standard tier already; paid ads compress margin further and compete directly with incumbents' SEO-honed landing pages. Revisit once organic/B2B channels are proving out unit economics.

## 4. Messaging and trust signals

Every competitor's marketing collapses to the same anxiety: "will this actually work at the border/check-in/embassy." Peregrin's messaging should lead with the same three signals the market has already validated, rather than trying to invent new ones:

- **"Real reservation, independently verifiable"** — the booking reference can be checked directly against the airline, not just trusted on Peregrin's word. This is the actual product differentiator versus a forged PDF, and it's now backed by a genuinely professional document (the PDF was rebuilt this session specifically to close the credibility gap against a competitor sample).
- **Transparency about what the document is and isn't** — "a real held reservation, not a ticket, unless the status shows confirmed." Stated plainly rather than buried, both because it's the compliant posture and because over-promising here is exactly what erodes trust in this category.
- **Speed** — the whole flow (search → hold → branded document) is already near-instant in the demo; worth quantifying ("under 60 seconds") once live, matching how every competitor markets on delivery speed.
- **For B2B partners specifically:** white-label credibility (their branding, their support contact, Peregrin invisible to the end customer) and margin (undercut current GDS-access cost via direct Duffel integration).

## 5. Country and language targeting

Supply-side priority (where the agency/partner outreach is strongest): Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia — matches the existing shortlist work and Duffel's stated go-live route coverage. Expand next to Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam.

Demand-side priority (where travellers actually need this product): the same Southeast Asian corridor for enforcement of onward-ticket rules (Thailand, Indonesia/Bali especially, Philippines, Vietnam), plus tourist-visa contexts for the US, Schengen area, Australia, and New Zealand. Digital nomads and backpackers doing repeated border/visa runs are the core buyer profile across all of these.

Language sequencing already matches this: English (default), Spanish (LATAM demand, underserved by incumbents), Russian, Hindi. Portuguese and Bahasa Indonesia are the next logical additions given the identified SEO gap.

## 6. Launch sequencing tied to milestones

- **Now – 2 weeks:** finish Stripe integration (the actual blocker to taking money), get Duffel go-live confirmed, close the loop on email delivery, continue B2B outreach follow-ups, complete affiliate signups.
- **Weeks 2–6:** first B2B partner conversations converting (target: one signed pilot), affiliate links live and tracked, first long-tail/non-English content pages published.
- **Months 2–4:** expand agency shortlist to additional countries, begin premium ticketed-tier rollout, start community participation (Reddit/forums) now that there's a live product and, ideally, an early partner or two to point to as proof.
- **Months 4–6+:** B2B API/webhook layer built for partners who want direct integration rather than white-label PDFs, programmatic SEO page count scaling, revisit paid channels only if unit economics are validated.

## 7. What this doesn't cover

This program assumes the B2B-first, compliance-conscious model described in the Business Model doc — not the original retail-agency outreach approach, which was reviewed and found to carry real legal and contractual risk. Any marketing activity should stay consistent with that: no messaging that implies guaranteed ticketing when the product is a hold, no positioning that encourages misrepresenting intent to an airline or immigration authority, and country-specific legal review before marketing spend scales into a new jurisdiction.
